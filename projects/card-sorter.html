Overview

A machine-vision system for Magic: The Gathering cards. Each card is photographed, read with OCR, classified by printing and variant, matched against a card database, and routed by the sorting mechanism, with results logged to a backend.

Problem

Sorting large trading-card collections by hand is slow, and similar printings are easy to confuse.

Engineering objectives

  • Identify each card from a camera image.
  • Distinguish printings and variants of the same card.
  • Decide a sorting destination and drive the mechanism.
  • Log every identification for review.

System architecture

  1. Camera
  2. Image preprocessing
  3. OCR / variant detection
  4. Card database lookup
  5. Sorting decision
  6. Machine control
  7. Logging / backend

Simplified, illustrative architecture

Hardware & build

A camera station feeds a card-handling mechanism controlled by the sorting logic. The illustration on this page is a concept drawing, not the machine itself.

Technical approach

A camera captures each card; OCR and variant classification identify it, a card database lookup confirms it, and the result drives the sorting mechanism.

Engineering considerations

  • Consistent lighting so text and set symbols stay readable, including on foil cards.
  • Telling apart printings that share the same name.
  • Keeping recognition in step with the mechanical card feed.
